SUDARSHAN TASK FORCE · BRAND DIRECTIONS · STAGE 1
Three visual directions for STF
Each direction covers brand concept, logo system, color tokens (with dark-mode suggestions), typography, a mini UI kit, and two key screens: the mobile employee check-in and the admin attendance dashboard. Built from STF Pack 01 — practical, calm, accountable; no surveillance styling. Options: 1a Sahay · 1b Dhruv · 1c Disha · comparison.
1a
Sahay — Warm & Human
CONCEPT
Work runs on trust between owner and worker — STF as a steady, supportive hand, not a monitor.
Warm surfaces, generous rounding, and a terracotta–green pairing rooted in Indian workplaces (brick, counters, ledgers). It reads as approachable for first-time app users while the deep green keeps admin views serious. Best where employee adoption is the main risk.
PERSONALITY
Supportive
Respectful
Grounded
Everyday
LOGO SYSTEM — two arcs meeting to complete one circle: owner and worker closing the loop of a day's work. A center point marks the record. Reads clearly at 16px.

Comparison & recommendation
DIRECTION
1a Sahay — Warm & Human
1b Dhruv — Utilitarian
1c Disha — Fintech-Clean
FEELING
Supportive, familiar, human — like a helpful colleague
Tough, direct, signage-clear — like good equipment
Precise, credible, premium — like a banking app
BEST AUDIENCE
Shops, service teams, first-time app users; adoption-risk workforces
Warehouse, dispatch, delivery, field crews; outdoor screens
Owners and admins comparing SaaS; payroll-sensitive buyers
STRENGTH
Least surveillance-like; strongest employee dignity signal
Best glare/low-end-device legibility; unmistakable statuses
Scales cleanly to payroll, reports, and module admin; matches Brand Guidelines' indigo foundation
RISK
Can read as soft/consumer; weaker authority in payroll views
Uppercase + amber can feel stern; borderline "industrial HR"
Risks feeling generic-fintech if the logo and voice aren't kept distinctive
USE WHEN
Employee adoption is the #1 launch metric
Workforce is mostly field/warehouse operations
One system must serve workers and owner-buyers equally
Recommendation: 1c Disha is the strongest foundation. It follows the Brand Guidelines' suggested indigo base, carries authority in payroll and module-management views (where trust matters most), stays calm and non-surveillance-like on the employee side, and has the widest headroom as modules grow. Sahay's warmth and Dhruv's status-labeling discipline are worth borrowing later — but per your instruction, no directions are combined until you select one.
